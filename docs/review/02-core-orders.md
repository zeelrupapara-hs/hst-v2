# core-orders review

Scope: hst-core handler/orders.go, validate.go, routing.go, pending.go, money.go, util.go, dealing.go, dealers_online.go, model/trade.go, routing.go, retcode.go, internal/settings/settings.go, calendar.go (13 files, 4 635 lines) plus callees read for the traces: handler/nats.go, ticks.go, store.go, positions.go (Execute, closingOrder), floating_margin.go, positions_hedge.go, publish.go, load.go (orders, rules, managers), shards.go, internal/book; hst-server internal/server/v1/orders.go, dealing.go; docs/order-lifecycle.md. `go build ./...` and `go vet` clean.

## Summary
The validation chain matches docs/order-lifecycle.md gate for gate (plus an undocumented hedge-prohibit gate) and the routing walk is clean and readable. The dealer queue is the weak part: a dealer confirm of a pending/modify/cancel request re-INSERTs the order row, a reject or timeout of a modify request deletes the live order, and the request state set by the caller is clobbered. Two money-path defects are independent of dealing: `updateOrder` writes the extended volume into the legacy column (×10 000 on reload), and a symbol created with default margin rates (0) reserves no margin and skips the money check. Market orders fill at a client-named price under confirm-by-request rules in non-instant modes. Readability is good for a junior: short functions, one idea each; the dealing state machine and the two duplicated ActivationFlags constant sets are what would confuse them first.
